## Top 3 hard questions

**Q1. "Your own numbers say the winches can't lift your claw plus jeans. Does the robot work, and why is 450 g still the requirement?"**

Answer. Not yet, and the deck says so. At 1.06 A direct drive the four winches hold about 1.1 kg total in the worst case at the highest task pose, and 0.50 kg at the 0.4 m ceiling limit (FUNDAMENTALS §1). The 1 lb test load plus the 0.45 kg budget claw is 0.9 kg. The printed claw is estimated at 1.05-1.25 kg and has not been weighed, so claw plus 1 lb is about 1.5-1.7 kg and claw plus jeans 2.0-2.2 kg (memo). The options on record: Ø10 core, +25 % (1.38 kg); a 3:1 reduction at each winch, about 2.7× (~3 kg); or a 0.3-0.5 kg payload for the demo. The first step is a 15-minute luggage-scale pull test, because the 0.7 running-torque factor is assumed and the true torque could be up to 1.4× lower (two-phase rating); the result becomes decision D31. 450 g (R7) is the number the analysis assumed, not a weighed claw: v6 was 2.06 kg in CAD, the v7 head is 481 g in CAD, and weighing it is the next step.

**Q2. "At 1.06 A in a vented shell your chart shows 51 °C with no fin, under your 60 °C limit. Why a fin, and what have you measured?"**

Answer. The fin was sized when the plan was 1.35 A and a sealed shell (review model, no fin sealed: 67 °C at 1.0 A, 106 °C at 1.5 A). At the 1.06 A driver cap D30 says thermal margin is large, so the fin now buys margin (vented 51 to 41 °C, sealed 64 to 46 °C) and keeps the loaded PETG tab cooler, which is the creep concern. Every temperature is CALCULATED: coil resistance 1.8 Ω is assumed (1.5 Ω gives 0.83× the rise), h is 5-9 W/m²K, alloy and finish are unknown, and no thermometer reading exists. The planned test is a 60-minute hold on the vented shell with thermocouples on the motor case, fin and PETG tab, read at 60 minutes against 0.94 × the 41 °C prediction, pass below 60 °C. If bare is already fine, the test says the fin is not earning its place.

**Q3. "Your only stop is a power strip and cutting power drops the claw. What is the safety case, and what happens if a stepper skips?"**

Answer. It is a deliberate category-0 stop with no brake: cutting 12 V removes holding torque and the claw falls (review R2, T04). The accepted mitigation is that nobody stands under the robot while it moves, with a 1 lb first gate (D19) and a slow final approach (CR7). In software a stop never disables: every fault and pause holds torque, only a disarm confirmed with the claw supported releases it (D21), and nothing resumes after a reboot (D25). No safety-rated function is claimed (ISO 13849 and IEC 61508 are not claimed); a controlled stop or self-locking drive is a gen-3 item. Steppers can lose steps silently on a snag or shock (T01); the four-station test requires no step loss and at most 2 mm per cable, and the station board has an encoder port, but none of this has run on four stations and the control behaviour is simulated only (B2).
